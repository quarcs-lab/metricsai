## Chapter issues found (NOT fixed in the .qmd; awaiting the user's decision)

1. **13.6 DiD design is misdescribed.**
   - The chapter says high-treatment communities "got many new clinics".
   - The dataset label is `hightreat = 1 if community has clinic in 1993`.
   - The source, Tanaka (2014, AEJ: Policy), studies the 1994 abolition of user fees for young children and pregnant women, with exposure through existing clinic access.
2. **13.7 RD is party-level, not candidate-level.**
   - `vote` = Democratic vote share in the next election, and `margin` = Democratic margin of victory.
   - The chapter frames it as an individual senator's incumbency advantage.
   - The chapter's description "margin = vote share − 50%" is also wrong: margin runs from −100 to +100.
3. **RD effect written as "4.8%" / "~5%".**
   - Where: the printed output line, the interpretation, and the Key Takeaways.
   - It should be **percentage points**.
4. **"Robust across different specifications" and "Robust to model specification" (RD) are unsupported.**
   - The chapter fits only one global linear model with a common slope over margins −100 to +100, including 45 uncontested races.
5. **13.3: "1% increase in unemployment → 1.03% decrease in inflation" should be percentage points.**
6. **13.6 effect-size translations are not computed from the data.**
   - The claims are "0.52 SD", "25th → 45th percentile" and "6–12 months of growth".
   - WAZ is in reference-population SDs. The sample SD is 1.59, so the effect is about 0.33 sample SDs.
7. **13.6 "Evidence for validity" bullets are asserted, not shown.**
   - The bullets are "placement based on need" and "no other programs".
   - "Self-reported data" is questionable, since weight is measured.
8. **Key Takeaways header "Advanced Causal Methods (Case Studies 13.4–13.8)" is mislabelled.**
   - 13.4 (autos) is observational and descriptive.
9. **13.4 "Torque elasticity ≈ −0.10 to −0.15" is vague; the estimate is −0.141.**
   - Also, "Clustering increases SEs" is asserted but never shown. The slides show it: 6–9× versus HC1.
10. **13.5 overstatements:**
    - "R² is low — this is actually good!": R² is irrelevant to identification.
    - "balanced on all characteristics": true only in expectation.
    - "RAND ensured compliance": attrition and refusal are documented concerns.
    - "Higher cost-sharing → lower spending" glosses over the insignificant 50% plan (p = 0.55).
11. **13.8 "IV larger points to measurement error as the dominant source" is stated as a conclusion.**
    - It is one of several readings; LATE heterogeneity and exclusion violations are others.
12. **13.2 Cobb–Douglas: no issue.**
    - The HAC Wald test is correct. The old deck had dropped the HAC SEs; this is now fixed in the slides.
